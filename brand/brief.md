# Desk Studio, the brand brief

The input for the studio's own brand job (phase 4). The Brand Strategist, Typographer, Design System Lead and UI Designer work from this. Nothing here is final until Adi picks a direction.

## What changed on 2026-10-29

The dashboard and the site shots looked dull, nothing showed a fun dashboard, and the lamp wasn't working. So I redesigned the dashboard first and redid the brand to match it.

- Was: a desk slab with a lamp disc as the mark, a "DS" monogram as the app icon, one amber accent, no characters, no gradients, no robots and no mascot. Now: every desk is a toy bot at its own desk, the mark is that bot's head with one lens eye, and the six trims of the site film carry the tiers (amber Manager, pink directors, sky seniors, green builders, blue for information, orange for what needs you). A soft dusk light is allowed.
- Was: Inter 600 for the wordmark. Now: Inter 800, still the same face as the film.
- The lamp, the slab and the disc are retired everywhere. The old kit's pages (`card.html`, `hero.html`, `marks.html`, `sting.html` and the old `sheet.html`) are in git history; `sheet.html` now shows the new kit.
- The visual starting points and the "Not this" list below are kept as they were written for the first brand job, on 2026-10-18.

## What it is

A design studio inside the coding agent people already use. Forty-two desks, each a real role with a brief, staffed per job by a Design Manager. Work goes through roughs, a pick, polish, proof on real pixels, and a sign-off against an eight-axis rubric. Cheap models make, strong models judge, and the user runs it all from a local dashboard: pause, swap a role, write a desk a note, pick the rough yourself.

## Who it is for

A developer or a founder who ships things and has no designer. They want launch cards, a landing page, an email, a deck, a clip, and they want it to look like a studio made it. They already pay for a coding agent and a model plan or two.

## The hook

"Your own design studio."

Was "Forty designers at your desk." until 2026-10-18 23:11 CDT, when Adi changed it because it isn't true: the desks are roles, not designers and not people. At 23:16 CDT he kept the subline and set the goal: adoption and contributors, and nobody reading the page as another ad. So "free" stays in the subline only, and what proves it is the open line and the link to the code, never a price word in the headline.

Subline: a full design studio inside the coding agent you already use. One command, free, nothing leaves your machine but the model calls you already make.

Open line, under the install command: "Open source, MIT. No account, no sign-up." Secondary link: "Read the code", to the GitHub repo.

## The three beats under it

1. It reaches studio quality and shows you the score.
2. On the plans you already have, cheap models make and strong models judge, and you see what each desk cost.
3. You run the dashboard.

## Proof available

- Real outputs from real runs with their rubric scores (evals/golden).
- A real cost line: a LinkedIn card at 8.6 for 0.48 at list price, directors 0.25, roughs 0.22, the Haiku polish round a cent.
- The dashboard itself, recorded.

## Voice

Plain, confident, short. Claims, not descriptions. Numbers as figures. No hype words, no openers, no exclamation marks, no comparisons to anything.

## Visual starting points, for the three directions

- The dashboard is the product shot. The brand has to look right as a dark UI with 42 small desks, and as a hero on a white page.
- One typeface, two weights, by default. Geist is the obvious developer-tool face and it ships in the plugin; a direction may argue for Inter or a serif pairing.
- One accent. The test card used amber on near-black and it worked. A direction may keep it, warm it, or replace it.
- A mark that works at 16px on a tab and at 400px on a hero: a desk, a lamp, a registration mark, a monogram, or type only.
- A motion language the dashboard uses: how a desk lights, how the pick ring draws, how the scorecard fills.

## Not this

Blobs, gradients, glass, robots, sparkles, anything that says AI. Stock illustration. A mascot.

## Deliverables of the brand job

Three directions, each a full kit folder to the brand-kit standard: mark and wordmark SVG, lockups, palette by job with ratios, type, spacing, motion language, the sheet, and the three applications (a social card, a site hero at desktop and phone, a three-second sting). The hero's headline is the hook and the card carries one of the three beats. Adi picks one. Then the chosen kit is polished and signed off into `brand/` with `DESIGN.md`, `motion.md`, `palette.css` and `README.md`, and the dashboard (`dashboard/public/style.css`) is restyled to it as a separate step after the pick.
