# Standard: copy

The words on everything the studio makes, and the deliverable itself when the ask is words: headlines, CTAs, subject lines, captions, scripts, a strategy, a brief. Written for the surface it lands on, to its length, in the brand's voice, and judged on the copy axis of the rubric plus this file. Copy-only jobs skip proof and run roughs, pick, polish and sign-off on the text.

## What top studio copy does

- Makes a claim or states a fact. A headline says what is true or what the reader gets, in words they would use. It never describes the category ("A design tool") or greets ("Welcome to").
- Says what happens next. A CTA is a verb plus the thing: "Start a free project", "Read the plan", "Get the file". The reader can predict the next screen from the button.
- Uses plain words. Short words over long, verbs over nouns, the specific over the general. A number is a plain figure with its unit and its source.
- Earns every word. Cut a word and the line gets weaker, or the word goes. The length tables below are ceilings, not targets.
- Writes in the brand's voice from `DESIGN.md`: the three adjectives and the example line. Without a brand, the house voice: direct, understated, concrete, a little dry.
- Keeps one idea per unit. One headline, one claim. One paragraph, one point. One slide, one thought. One caption, one line to say.
- Leads with the point. The first sentence of a body, the first line of a caption, the first beat of a script carries the idea. Context comes after, if at all.
- Reads aloud without a stumble. No stacked adjectives, no noun strings over two, no sentence over 25 words on a screen.
- Matches across the piece. The same term for the same thing everywhere, the same CTA words each time it appears, one tense, one person.
- Checks every fact. A number, a name, a claim about what the product does, each traced to a source the Copy Editor can see. Unverifiable claims are cut, not softened.

## Voice

- Sentence case everywhere, including headlines, buttons and nav. Title Case only when the brand asks.
- Second person for the reader ("you"), first person plural for the brand ("we") only when a person is speaking. Products are "it".
- Contractions are fine. Exclamation marks are not. Questions in a headline only when the body answers them in the next line.
- Numbers as figures from 10 up and for every measurement, price and percentage. Under 10 as words in prose, figures on a card.
- Punctuation: full stops on sentences, none on headlines, labels or buttons. Commas, not dashes, to join. No semicolons. Quotation marks for actual quotes only.
- Jargon only when the reader uses it. An acronym is expanded the first time unless the audience owns it.

Ceilings. Count words, not characters, except where the platform counts characters.

## Banned words and moves

Words: seamless, robust, leverage, elevate, unlock, supercharge, empower, delve, utilise, synergy, cutting-edge, next-generation, revolutionary, game-changing, world-class, best-in-class, powerful (for software), effortless, frictionless, holistic, innovative, solution (for a product), journey (for using a product), unleash, transform (without saying into what), "and more", "and much more".

Moves: "Welcome to", "Introducing", "We're excited to announce", "Learn more" as a CTA, "Click here", "Submit", a headline that is the product category, a rhetorical question with no answer, a tidy triad for rhythm's sake, "not X, but Y" balances, a closer that restates the opener, an exclamation mark, an emoji in brand copy, Title Case headlines where the brand did not ask, a statistic with no source, a superlative with no measure, a comparison with another product or tool, any mention of how the copy was written, lorem ipsum or placeholder text in anything handed back.

## File contract

For copy as part of a visual job: every string lives in the brief's copy block, verbatim, and the builder puts it in the file as real DOM text, unchanged. The Copy Editor's return names every string changed with the reason. Proof checks `--must-appear` strings against the render.

For a copy-only job: one Markdown file per deliverable under the job's out folder, named for the surface (`headlines.md`, `captions.md`, `subjects.md`, `script.md`, `strategy.md`), with one option per line under a heading per unit, the recommended one first and marked, the word or character count beside each, and a short "why" line only where the choice is not obvious. Roughs are three files with three directions of voice. The pick and polish run on the text, the Creative Director scores copy, hierarchy (of the argument) and brand fit, and the other axes are marked n/a.

Sizes for this deliverable, by name: none. Lengths come from the table above.
