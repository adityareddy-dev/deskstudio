# The bar

Every visual deliverable is scored on eight axes, 0 to 10 each, by the Creative Director looking at the rendered screenshots, never at the code. It passes when every axis is 8 or above and the average is 8.5 or above. Under 8 on any axis is a fail no matter the average.

Score what is on the screen. A 10 is work a top studio would put its name on without a change. An 8 is work that would ship at a good studio after one small note. A 6 has a problem a client would point at. Under 5 is template work.

| Axis | 10 looks like | Common reasons it is under 8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hierarchy | One thing reads first, one second, the rest supports. The eye path is obvious from across the room | two things fight for first, the headline and the logo at the same weight, a CTA that reads before the message |
| Typography | A scale with a reason, two weights, tight headlines, open body, consistent line length, optical alignment of the big type | three sizes that are too close, a headline at body leading, widows, a weight used once, letterspacing on lowercase body |
| Spacing and rhythm | Everything sits on one grid, margins equal to the content's importance, groups read as groups because of space, not lines | uneven gutters, a block touching an edge, equal space between unrelated things, space filled because it was there |
| Colour | A palette with a job per colour, one accent used for one purpose, contrast that reads at a glance, dark and light handled on purpose | two accents, a gradient doing the work of a layout, grey text on colour, colour used for decoration only |
| Composition | The canvas is used, the layout has a shape, asymmetry with intent, one focal point, the size it was made for | everything centred, a box inside a box, the layout ignores the aspect ratio, decorative shapes with no job, an empty band over a third of the canvas that nothing shaped |
| Originality | It could only be this brand's piece. A move a template would not make. Restraint read as confidence | the first layout anyone would make, stock gradient blobs, an icon where a word would do, every element is a card |
| Brand fit | The palette, the type and the voice are the brand's, used the way the brand would use them, the logo at the right size and clear space | wrong weight of the brand type, the accent used as a background, the logo as a decoration, a tone the brand would not use |
| Copy | Every word earns its place, the headline makes a claim, the CTA says what happens next, nothing generic | "Welcome to", "Introducing", "seamless", a headline that describes instead of claims, a CTA that says "Learn more" |

The Creative Director's return for a sign-off is always:

```
scores: hierarchy 9, typography 8, spacing 9, colour 9, composition 8, originality 8, brand 9, copy 9
average: 8.6
pass: yes
notes:
- <axis under 9>: <what to change, exactly, in one line>
```

Notes are instructions, not observations. "Headline up 24px, weight 700 to 600, cut the subhead to eight words" is a note. "Typography feels heavy" is not.
